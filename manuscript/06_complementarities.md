# Complementarity: structures one programme supplies and another needs {#sec:complementarities}

The {{REGISTRY_COMPLEMENTARITIES}} complementarities are not identifications and
not failures. Each is a case where one programme supplies a structure that
another needs and does not have, without either reducing to the other. They are
the constructive part of the paper: most of them are geometric, the rest are a
learning identity, a potential flow and a resistance metric, and two need all
three vocabularies at once.

## Synergetics supplies the accounting in which the carrier's geometry is exact {#sec:quadray}

The carrier of [@def:carrier] is a graph. To draw it, to count its shells, and to
state the volume of the polyhedra it bounds, a graph is not enough: a metric is
needed, and the choice of metric decides which numbers come out exact. Synergetics
supplies one in which they all do.

::: {#prop:quadray-ivm .proposition}
The twelve quadray neighbour moves of [@eq:neighbours] are distinct, all have
squared norm {{IVM_NEIGHBOR_SQUARED_DISTANCE}}, and their minimum pairwise squared
distance is also {{IVM_NEIGHBOR_SQUARED_DISTANCE}}, so the twelve form a kissing
configuration of radius $\sqrt{2}$. The coordination shells, enumerated, have
`{{IVM_SHELL_COUNTS}}` sites for $k = 0,\dots,4$ and cumulative counts
`{{IVM_CUMULATIVE_COUNTS}}`. The tetravolume table `{{IVM_VOLUME_RATIOS}}` is
derived exactly: each solid is the convex hull of an explicit integer vertex set
built from the lattice -- the tetrahedron from the four axes, the cube from the
axes and their negatives, the octahedron from the pairwise axis sums, the
cuboctahedron from the twelve neighbours, the rhombic dodecahedron as their
Voronoi cell, and the truncated octahedron by cutting the frequency-three
octahedron at the thirds of its edges -- and its volume, computed in rational
arithmetic, is divided by the unit tetrahedron's.
:::

The complementarity is exact and one-directional. OPH's dynamics does not need
quadray coordinates, and the FEP does not need a lattice. But the moment the
carrier is drawn, or its shells are counted as geometry rather than as graph
distance, the quadray convention is what makes the statements exact rather than
approximate. The derivation matters because a table is a claim: the cube's
three and the truncated octahedron's ninety-six are conventions about *which*
cube and *which* truncated octahedron (the cube whose face diagonals are the
prime vector, and the truncated octahedron whose edge is), and only a vertex set
fixes that choice. [@fig:ivm] shows the shells and the volume ratios.

![The IVM accounting. Left: shell sizes `10k^2 + 2` for $k = 0,\dots,8$ with the
cumulative count overlaid; the first shell is the twelve-around-one
configuration. Right: tetravolume ratios on a logarithmic scale, with the unit
tetrahedron at one, so that the cube, octahedron, rhombic dodecahedron,
cuboctahedron and truncated octahedron are all integers. Generated by
`scripts/make_figures.py` from `geometry/lattice.py`.](../output/figures/fig_ivm.png){#fig:ivm width=100%}

The kissing number is the point at which the two programmes touch. The carrier has
{{CARRIER_PORTS}} ports of degree {{CARRIER_DEGREE}}; the IVM has {{IVM_NEIGHBOR_MOVES}}
neighbours at squared distance {{IVM_NEIGHBOR_SQUARED_DISTANCE}}; and the
geometric icosahedron's adjacency is isomorphic to the derived carrier adjacency,
which the probe checks by constructing a witness. The coincidence is not a
numerological one: twelve is the kissing number in three dimensions
[@schutte1953dreizehn; @conway1999sphere], and both arrangements attain it. They
attain it differently, which the `kissing-twelve` probe measures exactly: the
cuboctahedral arrangement is rigid, its spheres touching their neighbours, while
the icosahedral one has slack, its squared edge over squared circumradius being
$2 - 2\sqrt{5}/5 \approx {{KISSING_ICOSAHEDRON_EDGE_RATIO_SQUARED}}$ rather than
{{KISSING_CUBOCTAHEDRON_EDGE_RATIO_SQUARED}} ([@sec:cuboctahedron]). The carrier
is the loose arrangement; the lattice is the tight one.

## The same quadratic sequence appears three times {#sec:subdivision}

Fuller's geodesic subdivision and Goldberg's dual construction produce the same
pair of quadratic sequences, and the IVM shell count is one of them.

::: {#prop:subdivision .proposition}
For every frequency $f \ge 1$: the geodesic subdivision of the icosahedron has
$10f^{2}+2$ vertices, $30f^{2}$ edges and $20f^{2}$ faces; its Goldberg dual has
$20f^{2}$ vertices, $30f^{2}$ edges and $10f^{2}+2$ faces. Hence the vertex count
of one is the face count of the other, and both are the IVM shell count of
[@eq:shell].
:::

![Three appearances of one sequence. The geodesic vertex count `10f^2 + 2`, the
Goldberg face count, and the IVM shell count are the same function of the
frequency, while the geodesic face count and the Goldberg vertex count are
`20f^2`. The plot shows both sequences against $f$ and annotates the duality.
Generated by `scripts/make_figures.py` from `geometry/subdivision.py`.](../output/figures/fig_subdivision.png){#fig:subdivision width=85%}

This is the clearest complementarity in the paper, and the least mysterious.
Duality swaps vertices and faces, so the two counts trade places, and Euler's
formula with twelve five-valent vertices fixes the rest. The IVM shell count is
the same quadratic for a combinatorial reason: the frequency-$f$ shell of the
vector equilibrium has eight triangular and six square faces of frequency $f$, and
cutting each square along a diagonal turns it into the frequency-$f$ geodesic
icosahedron on the same vertex set, which is the move Fuller's jitterbug makes
continuously [@fuller1975synergetics; @verheyen1989jitterbug]. The geodesic
counts are the $T = f^{2}$ case of the Caspar-Klug classification of icosahedral
shells [@caspar1962physical; @coxeter1973regular]. What Synergetics adds is the
insistence that the *volume* accounting is exact in the same convention, which is
what makes the subdivision usable as a discretisation rather than only as a
drawing.

## Energy-based learning supplies the gradient the repair law lacks {#sec:equilibrium-propagation}

Equilibrium propagation is the cleanest case in which an energy is not merely a
bound but a computational device: perturb the output, let the system relax, and
read the gradient from the difference in the relaxed energy
[@scellier2017equilibrium]. It belongs to energy-based learning rather than to
the FEP, which reaches the same territory through predictive coding: inference
by settling a precision-weighted prediction-error energy approximates
back-propagation [@whittington2017approximation; @millidge2022predictive]. The
probe is a scalar energy model, chosen so that every quantity has a closed form.

::: {#prop:ep .proposition}
At perturbation $\beta$, the closed-form contrast is {{EP_CLOSED_FORM}} and the
relaxed contrast is {{EP_RELAXED}}, against an exact gradient of
{{EP_EXACT_GRADIENT}}. The closed-form error is {{EP_CLOSED_FORM_ERROR}} and the
distance to the gradient is {{EP_GRADIENT_DISTANCE}}, matching the predicted
$O(\beta)$ scaling.
:::

The complementarity is that an energy provides the *objective* and the
relaxation dynamics provides the *implementation*, and the identity between them
is exact to the stated order in the perturbation. It needs a symmetric
interaction, which is why the directed case of [@sec:directed] is an obstruction.
Neither OPH's repair rule nor Synergetics' accounting gives such an identity, and
neither needs one.

## Selection supplies a potential flow, for symmetric payoffs {#sec:replicator}

::: {#prop:replicator .proposition}
For a symmetric payoff $A$, the replicator field
$\dot x_i = x_i\bigl((Ax)_i - x^{\top}Ax\bigr)$ equals $M(x)\nabla F$ with
$F = \tfrac{1}{2}x^{\top}Ax$ and mobility $M = \operatorname{diag}(x) - xx^{\top}$,
at every point checked, with the direct potential rate {{REPLICATOR_RATE}}
agreeing with the rate computed through the mobility and the field norm
{{REPLICATOR_FIELD_NORM}} at the reported point. For a non-symmetric payoff the
same identity fails at every point checked.
:::

The replicator equation for a symmetric (partnership) game is a gradient flow
in the Shahshahani metric [@shahshahani1979new; @taylor1978evolutionary;
@hofbauer1998evolutionary], and the mean payoff is a Lyapunov function for it.
The probe checks the identity, not merely the sign, and checks its boundary: with
an asymmetric payoff the field is still $M(x)Ax$, but no potential reproduces it.

The relevance to the three programmes runs through inference. The discrete
replicator equation is Bayes' rule, with types as hypotheses and fitness as
likelihood [@harper2009replicator], so the FEP's posterior updating and
selection share one dynamical form, and this is the one place where a potential
is unambiguously a Lyapunov function of a dynamics that appears in population
biology rather than in neuroscience. It supplies a template: a
synthesis that wants a free-energy descent for OPH's repairs should look for the
metric in which the repair field is a gradient, and the replicator case shows what
such a metric looks like when it exists. Registered as `replicator-potential`.

## The carrier's own solid has an exact algebraic volume {#sec:tetravolume}

The tetravolume table of [@sec:quadray] is a table of integers, and the carrier's
own solid is not in it. It has an exact volume all the same, and the fact that the
volume is exact while being algebraic rather than integral is the sharpest
statement of what the synergetic convention buys.

::: {#prop:ico-volume .proposition}
The regular icosahedron whose edge is the prime vector has tetravolume
{{ICOSAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME}}, equal to
$\tfrac{5}{2}\bigl(3\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{10}\bigr)$ exactly, and the square of that
tetravolume is {{ICOSAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME_SQUARED}}, which is exactly
$175 + 75\sqrt{5}$. Its twelve vertices are
{{ICOSAHEDRON_DEGREE}}-valent, and its twenty triangles are all congruent.
:::

The probe computes the volume twice. The exact route places the icosahedron of
edge two at $(0, \pm 1, \pm\varphi)$ and its cyclic permutations, with
$\varphi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$, finds its twenty facets by an exact supporting-plane
test, and sums signed tetrahedra in the field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$; dividing by
the regular tetrahedron of the same edge gives the tetravolume as $\sqrt{2}$
times `{{ICOSAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME_OVER_SQRT2}}` exactly, with no rounding anywhere.
The floating-point route applies the divergence theorem to the measured surface
and divides by the same tetrahedron, and the two agree to the last digit, so the
agreement is an identity rather than a tolerance chosen to make it hold.

Two details of the computation are worth recording because they are corrections
rather than conveniences. The first is that a face list is an *unordered* set of
triangles: ten of the twenty faces enumerated from the vertex set come out
facing inward ({{ICOSAHEDRON_FACES_REORIENTED}} of them), and the signed sum
cancels exactly before they are reoriented. The second is that the tetravolume
convention is a convention: changing the unit tetrahedron rescales every entry in
the table, and the claim made here is the ratio the convention defines and not an
absolute volume.

Why this is a complementarity rather than an identification is now visible. OPH
supplies the graph, and the graph's faces are triangles; Synergetics supplies the
convention in which those triangles have exactly computable volumes; and neither
programme as written states the volume of the solid its own graph bounds. The
table's integral entries and the carrier's algebraic entry belong to the same
accounting, which is what makes the disagreement of [@sec:cuboctahedron] a
statement about solids and not about conventions.

## The densest packing in three dimensions has the twelve-around-one cell {#sec:packing}

::: {#prop:packing .proposition}
The Voronoi cell of the twelve neighbours has {{VORONOI_VERTICES}} vertices,
{{VORONOI_FACES}} rhombic faces and {{VORONOI_EDGES}} edges, with Euler
characteristic two and no orientation flips. Its tetravolume is
{{PACKING_CELL_TETRAVOLUME}}, and the ball of radius half the prime vector
inscribed in it has tetravolume {{PACKING_BALL_TETRAVOLUME}}, so the packing
fraction is {{PACKING_DENSITY}}, equal to $\pi/(3\sqrt{2})$.
:::

This is the paper's clearest case of a number that can be *derived* instead of
read. The tetravolume of the cell is computed from the halved squared norm at each
of its fourteen vertices, using the same quadratic form that makes the neighbour
distances exact, and it arrives at six: the same six the synergetic table lists
for the rhombic dodecahedron. A reader who suspects that a table entry is a
stylised fact can therefore check it by an independent route, which is the
pattern this package uses wherever a source table has consequences.

The density is then derived rather than quoted, and it is exact. The cell has
integer vertices, so its volume is computed in rational arithmetic and its
tetravolume is exactly {{PACKING_CELL_TETRAVOLUME}}; the inscribed ball has radius
$\sqrt{2}$, half the prime vector, so its tetravolume is exactly $\pi\sqrt{2}$;
and the density is their ratio, $\pi\sqrt{2}/6 = \pi/(3\sqrt{2}) = {{PACKING_DENSITY}}$.
That number is the Kepler density of the face-centred cubic arrangement, of which
the IVM's twelve neighbours are the local picture. It is optimal among lattice
packings [@gauss1840seeber] and among all packings
[@hales2005kepler; @hales2017formal]. The complementarity is that Synergetics'
twelve-around-one is not a drawing convention but the local configuration of the
densest packing in three dimensions.

The carrier does not inherit that optimality, and the same cell is the reason
the disagreement of [@sec:cuboctahedron] is not a quibble. The densest packing's
kissing arrangement is the rigid cuboctahedral one, whose cell is the rhombic
dodecahedron of tetravolume six; the carrier's solid is the loose icosahedral
arrangement, of tetravolume ${{ICOSAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME}}$, with a
{{ICOSAHEDRON_DEGREE}}-regular graph. Both put twelve spheres around one, and they
are different objects.

## Three number systems and no common unit {#sec:currencies}

::: {#prop:currencies .proposition}
The three programmes' exact quantities live in different number systems. The
FEP's divergence for one IVM step is the integer {{KL_ONE_STEP}} nats (with unit
covariance in the default embedding); the carrier's normalised spectral gap, a
relaxation rate of OPH's mean repair operator, is the quadratic surd
$(5-\sqrt{5})/60 \approx {{CARRIER_SHELL_GAP}}$; and Synergetics' table is
integral, with the twelve-neighbour cell at {{PACKING_CELL_TETRAVOLUME}}, while
the carrier's own solid lies in $\sqrt{2}\,\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$.
:::

This is one of the two entries in the registry that need all three vocabularies
at once -- the other is the conjugation norm of [@sec:conjugation] -- and both
are complementarities rather than identifications for that reason. The three
quantities are attached to different objects (a mean shift, a relaxation, a
solid), so none is the "cost of a step" in another's terms, and no common unit
converts one into another: an integer, a surd and an element of a biquadratic
field cannot be made equal by rescaling. A reader who wants one number has to
choose a currency and state the exchange rate; a reader who wants the object has
three exact readings of it.

## The precision also carries a resistance metric {#sec:resistance}

::: {#prop:resistance .proposition}
Ground the Laplacian at one port and solve $L x = e_i - e_j$ over the rationals
[@klein1993resistance]:
the effective resistance across a seam is {{RESISTANCE_ADJACENT}}, at distance
two {{RESISTANCE_DISTANCE_TWO}}, and across the carrier
{{RESISTANCE_ANTIPODAL}}, over {{RESISTANCE_PAIR_COUNTS}} pairs in the three
classes, summing to a Kirchhoff index of {{RESISTANCE_KIRCHHOFF_INDEX}}.
:::

Neither programme asks for this number, which is what makes it a complement
rather than an identification. In the Gaussian reading it is the variance of a
port's potential difference, so it is available for free to anyone holding the
precision, and it is invisible to both programmes' own vocabularies: OPH never
forms it, and the FEP's free energy does not depend on it. The sum is the useful
part. The three classes contribute {{RESISTANCE_KIRCHHOFF_INDEX}} in total, and
the same total is twelve times the reciprocal spectrum sum
{{GMRF_RECIPROCAL_SPECTRUM_SUM}} from [@sec:gmrf], which is a cross-check between
a rational system solved pair by pair and a closed-form spectral identity. The
agreement is exact and the two routes share no arithmetic. Registered as
`effective-resistance`.

## The twelve-around-one cell and the cuboctahedron are duals {#sec:dual-cells}

::: {#prop:dual-cells .proposition}
The cell of the twelve neighbours and the cuboctahedron are dual edge for edge:
{{VORONOI_VERTICES}} vertices against {{CUBOCTAHEDRON_VERTICES}},
{{VORONOI_FACES}} faces against {{CUBOCTAHEDRON_FACES}}, and
{{CUBOCTAHEDRON_EDGES}} edges each, with Euler characteristic two each and
tetravolumes {{PACKING_CELL_TETRAVOLUME}} and {{CUBOCTAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME}}, the
cuboctahedron's to the cell's in the ratio {{DUAL_VOLUME_RATIO}}.
:::

The dual pair is derived twice, and the two derivations meet. The cell is built
from the constraints of the twelve lattice sites, and the cuboctahedron from the
neighbouring sites themselves; the cuboctahedron is also the cube's line graph,
which the probe asserts by an explicit isomorphism search over the twelve
vertices rather than by comparing degrees. [@sec:packing] uses the cell for a
density; this claim records that its partner is the other solid Synergetics names
for the same arrangement, and that the two accountings are related by exactly
{{DUAL_VOLUME_RATIO}}.

## Conjugation fixes the counts and moves the volumes {#sec:conjugation}

::: {#prop:conjugation .proposition}
Conjugating $\sqrt{5}$ while fixing $\sqrt{2}$ leaves the counting invariants
alone and moves the geometric ones. The spanning-tree number
{{CARRIER_TREE_NUMBER}} is a rational integer and is fixed; the icosahedron's
tetravolume {{ICOSAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME}}, $\sqrt{2}$ times
`{{ICOSAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME_OVER_SQRT2}}`, maps to
{{ICOSAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME_CONJUGATE}}, and the two multiply to exactly
{{TETRAVOLUME_GALOIS_NORM}}, computed in field arithmetic.
:::

This is the sharpest statement the paper can make about the difference between
its two kinds of claim, and it is why [@sec:scope] separates them. A count over a
finite complex is an integer, so the Galois symmetry of the quadratic field
cannot see it; a volume in the same field is not an integer, and the symmetry
moves it to a value that no solid of the table has. The product of the two is an
integer, {{TETRAVOLUME_GALOIS_NORM}}, so the conjugation is a check rather than a
curiosity: a transcription error in either volume would break a product that is
exactly rational. Registered as `conjugation-norm`.

## What the complements add up to

The {{REGISTRY_COMPLEMENTARITIES}} complements partition the labour cleanly.
Synergetics supplies a metric, a volume convention and an exact packing in which
the carrier's geometry is computable rather than merely drawable. The FEP
supplies a functional and a bound, and, through predictive coding and the
replicator's reading as inference, reaches two identities -- the energy-based
learning gradient and the Shahshahani potential flow -- that make the functional
computationally and dynamically meaningful. OPH supplies a finite substrate and
an explicit local update that neither of the others has.

Each complement is stated as a numbered proposition: the quadray accounting
([@prop:quadray-ivm]), the subdivision identity ([@prop:subdivision]), the
learning identity ([@prop:ep]), the replicator metric ([@prop:replicator]), the
carrier's solid ([@prop:ico-volume]), the packing ([@prop:packing]), the three
number systems ([@prop:currencies]), the resistance metric ([@prop:resistance]), the
dual pair ([@prop:dual-cells]) and the conjugation check ([@prop:conjugation]).

The synthesis that the complements suggest is therefore not a reduction but an
assignment: the carrier is OPH's, the functional is the FEP's, and the accounting
is Synergetics'. Two of the complements add a reading neither programme asks
for and both can use: the precision's own resistance metric, and the
conjugation that separates the counts from the volumes. [@sec:scope] states
the limits of that assignment.
