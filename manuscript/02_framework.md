# Carrier and comparison models {#sec:framework}

## The carrier

We start from OPH's finite icosahedral carrier. A Gaussian model on its graph
provides the inference comparison. Synergetics supplies a lattice and a unit
of volume for comparing the carrier's geometry with that of close packing.

::: {#def:carrier .definition}
The **carrier** is the simplicial complex $K=(V,E,F)$ whose oriented faces
come from OPH's port chart of the regular icosahedron [@oph]. Its vertices
are *ports*, and its edges are *seams*. A **load configuration** is a map
$x\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ with total load $\sum_v x_v$. A
configuration is **binary** when $x_v\in\{0,1\}$ for every port.
:::

OPH supplies the oriented faces. Two ports share a seam exactly when some face
contains both. We derive the adjacency from that rule and compare the vertex
sets of the resulting graph triangles with the supplied face vertex sets.
The original face orientations also provide the input to the rotation census
and the surface cohomology check.

::: {#prop:carrier .proposition}
The graph obtained from OPH's oriented faces is
{{CARRIER_DEGREE}}-regular on {{CARRIER_PORTS}} ports with {{CARRIER_SEAMS}} seams,
and the vertex sets of its {{CARRIER_FACES}} triangles are the generating
face vertex sets.
:::

The adjacency that results is listed in [@tbl:adjacency]. The labels matter
for controls that select particular ports or orient particular seams. Graph
invariants such as the Laplacian spectrum are invariant under relabelling.

The carrier's Laplacian is the standard graph Laplacian
$L = 5I - A$. Its spectrum is closed-form. The
algebraic connectivity is $5-\sqrt{5}$ [@fiedler1973connectivity], and the gap
normalised by the total degree $2\lvert E\rvert$ is $(5-\sqrt{5})/60$. A
uniform seam and a fair coin give the *mean* repair operator
$I - L/(2\lvert E\rvert)$ on port loads. The Laplacian also defines the
seam-disagreement energy
$\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\{a,b\}\in E}(x_a - x_b)^{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}x^{\top}Lx$.
Individual repairs lower or preserve the load energy defined in [@eq:energy].

::: {#prop:spectrum .proposition}
The carrier Laplacian has spectrum
$\{0,\ (5-\sqrt{5})^{\times 3},\ 6^{\times 5},\ (5+\sqrt{5})^{\times 3}\}$, so
$\lambda_1 = 5-\sqrt{5} \approx {{CARRIER_LAMBDA1}}$ and
$\lambda_1/(2\lvert E\rvert) = (5-\sqrt{5})/60 = {{CARRIER_SHELL_GAP}}$.
:::

The full icosahedral group acts on the ports through four pairwise inequivalent
irreducible subspaces, of dimensions one, three, five and three. An invariant
symmetric operator acts as a scalar on each subspace. For $5I-A$ these scalars
are distinct, so its eigenspaces are exactly the four subspaces
[@prop:symmetry]. [@fig:carrier] draws the carrier and its spectrum.

## OPH: a repair law on the seams

OPH's native dynamics is a local rule that moves integer load across a seam. On a
seam joining ports $a$ and $b$, with $d = x_a - x_b$ and a coin
$c\in\{0,1\}$, the amount moved is

$$
\delta \;=\; \frac{d - (2c-1)\,(\lvert d\rvert \bmod 2)}{2},
\qquad x_a \leftarrow x_a - \delta,\quad x_b \leftarrow x_b + \delta .
$$ {#eq:repair}

The rule is written this way, rather than as "move half the difference", because
it must stay in the integers: the parity correction is what keeps $\delta$
integral when $d$ is odd. The quadratic energy

$$
E(x) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V} x_v^{2}
$$ {#eq:energy}

is the load energy. It never increases under the native repair.

::: {#prop:descent .proposition}
Under [@eq:repair] the total load is conserved and
$\Delta E = -\bigl(d^{2} - (\lvert d\rvert \bmod 2)\bigr)/4$.
:::

At $\lvert d\rvert = 1$ the decrease is exactly zero. The coin selects either
a wait or a swap, and the swap changes the state at constant load energy.
This is the pathwise obstruction tested in [@sec:pr01].

::: {#def:shell .definition}
The **load shell** of total $q$ (OPH's shell, a level set of the total load; not
to be confused with the lattice's coordination shells of [@def:coordination]) is
the set
$\Sigma_q = \{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{V} : \sum_v x_v = q\}$. The shell of total
two has $\lvert\Sigma_2\rvert = \binom{13}{11} = {{SHELL_TOTAL_TWO}}$ configurations, of
which {{SHELL_BINARY}} are binary.
:::

The `shell-counts` probe checks these counts by enumerating the carrier's
load configurations.

## FEP: a bound on surprisal

For a given record $o$, let $s$ be the latent state in a generative model with
positive evidence $p(o)$. Take a recognition density $q(s)$ that is absolutely
continuous with respect to the posterior. The variational free energy is

$$
\mathcal{F}[q] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{q}\bigl[-\log p(o,s)\bigr]
\;-\; \mathbb{E}_{q}\bigl[-\log q(s)\bigr]
\;=\; D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl[q(s)\,\|\,p(s\mid o)\bigr] \;-\; \log p(o),
$$ {#eq:vfe}

so minimising $\mathcal{F}$ at fixed $o$ minimises the divergence to the
posterior. The negative free energy is a lower bound on the log evidence
[@friston2010; @friston2007synthese]. It is tight exactly at the posterior.
If the recognition family excludes the posterior, the residual divergence
measures the gap.

In the linear-Gaussian construction, partition the carrier Laplacian $L$ into
recorded ports $o$ and free ports $x$. The seam energy is
$E(x,o)=\tfrac12 z^\top Lz$, with $z=(x,o)$. At least one record grounds the
interior block $L_{xx}$. A record prior $\rho(o)=\tfrac12\epsilon\lVert o\rVert^2$
with $\epsilon>0$ grounds the full precision. Integrating the resulting
probability density over $x$ gives the record surprisal
[@mueller2026agreement, theorem 7]:

$$
-\ln p(o) \;=\; \tfrac12 o^\top (S+\epsilon I)o
       + \tfrac{d_o}{2}\ln(2\pi) - \tfrac12\ln\det(S+\epsilon I) ,
$$ {#eq:gaussian}

where $d_o$ is the number of record coordinates and $S$ is the Schur complement
of $L_{xx}$ in $L$. The interior Gaussian determinant appears in the raw
integral of $\exp(-E)$ and cancels in the probability density. With the prior
fixed, [@eq:gaussian] gives an absolute value and determines the minimum of
[@eq:vfe] over $q$. The probes compare this marginal route to an
energy-minus-entropy route that uses the full joint precision.

Partition the state into interior $i$, boundary $b$ and exterior $e$. The
boundary $b$ is a **Markov blanket** when it makes the interior and exterior
conditionally independent:

$$
p(i,e\mid b) \;=\; p(i\mid b)\,p(e\mid b).
$$ {#eq:blanket}

Equation [@eq:blanket] constrains the probability distribution. In the
stationary control in [@prop:stationary], a family of distinct drifts
preserves the same distribution and blanket. [@fig:ga-carrier] locates a
blanket on the carrier in the proper Gaussian model.

## Synergetics: tetravolume accounting

**Quadray** coordinates represent a point by four components with a common
offset. The representation identifies any tuples related by

$$
(a,b,c,d) \;\sim\; (a+t,\,b+t,\,c+t,\,d+t) \qquad \text{for every } t\in\mathbb{R},
$$ {#eq:quadray}

Component differences determine the point under [@eq:quadray]. The four axes map to
`(1, 1, 1)`, `(-1, 1, -1)`, `(-1, -1, 1)` and `(1, -1, -1)`, the
vertices of a regular tetrahedron. In the IVM sublattice, the nearest neighbours
of the origin are the distinct permutations of $(2,1,1,0)$.

$$
\mathcal{N} \;=\; \bigl\{\,\pi(2,1,1,0) \;:\; \pi \text{ a permutation}\,\bigr\},
\qquad \lvert\mathcal{N}\rvert = {{IVM_NEIGHBOR_MOVES}} .
$$ {#eq:neighbours}

The neighbour moves generate the IVM sublattice of integer quadrays. The first
shell is the *vector equilibrium* of Synergetics and the kissing arrangement
of the A3 root lattice, the face-centred cubic lattice
[@conway1999sphere; @schutte1953dreizehn]. The
neighbours are at squared distance {{IVM_NEIGHBOR_SQUARED_DISTANCE}} in the
default embedding, so this embedding's unit of length is half the edge of the
cube whose face diagonals are the neighbour moves; every Euclidean number below
is stated in that unit.

::: {#def:coordination .definition}
The **coordination shell** $S_k$ is the set of lattice sites at graph distance
exactly $k$ from the origin in the graph whose edges are the twelve neighbour
moves. Halving the embedded coordinates maps the lattice onto
$D_3 = \{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{3} : u_1+u_2+u_3 \equiv 0 \pmod 2\}$, where the graph
distance has the closed form

$$
g(u) \;=\; \max\bigl(\lVert u\rVert_{\infty},\ \tfrac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert_{1}\bigr),
\qquad
\lvert S_k\rvert \;=\; 10k^{2}+2 \quad (k \ge 1).
$$ {#eq:shell}

$g$ is the gauge function of the cuboctahedron whose vertices are the twelve
neighbours, so $S_k$ is the set of lattice points on the surface of the
frequency-$k$ vector equilibrium.
:::

The count $10k^{2}+2$ is the coordination sequence of the face-centred cubic
lattice [@conway1997lowdim] and QuadMath's shell formula [@quadmath]; the package
builds the shells by breadth-first expansion and checks the closed form $g$
against every site through shell {{SHELL_GAUGE_CHECKED_THROUGH}}. Coordination
shell sites lie on a common polyhedral boundary and can have several Euclidean
norms, as [@prop:shell-energy] shows.

The tetravolume of a lattice simplex is the absolute determinant of its three
edge vectors, each written in quadray coordinates with its fourth coordinate
subtracted from the other three, divided by four, so the regular tetrahedron of
the four axes has volume one. The six solids of Synergetics' table then have
integer volumes, {{IVM_VOLUME_RATIOS}}, and the package derives every entry
exactly from an explicit vertex set rather than reading it from the table
([@sec:quadray]). No square root appears in the accounting of the lattice's own
solids, which is the point of the convention [@fuller1975synergetics;
@fuller1979synergetics2; @edmondson1987fuller]; the one solid that needs one is
the carrier's ([@sec:tetravolume]).

::: {#prop:ivm .proposition}
The quadray lattice generated by [@eq:neighbours] has {{IVM_NEIGHBOR_MOVES}}
neighbour moves, all at squared distance {{IVM_NEIGHBOR_SQUARED_DISTANCE}},
coordination shells of $10k^{2}+2$ sites with cumulative counts
`{{IVM_CUMULATIVE_COUNTS}}` for $k=0,\dots,4$, and tetravolume ratios
`{{IVM_VOLUME_RATIOS}}`, each derived exactly.
:::
