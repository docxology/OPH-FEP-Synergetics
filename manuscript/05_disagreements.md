# Disagreement: the obstructions, each stated exactly {#sec:disagreements}

An obstruction is a result. Each of the {{REGISTRY_DISAGREEMENTS}} claims below
was proposed as an identification, and each fails in a way that can be stated
exactly. The section is ordered by how much the failure costs a synthesis: the
first failure is internal to OPH and blocks any confluence claim, the next two
block the identification of "free energy", the remainder block dynamical
identifications that the literature makes routinely, and the last two block
identifications that are made by counting rather than by dynamics.

## The native law is not confluent {#sec:pr01}

OPH records one of its own premises as obstructed: PR-01, its confluent
terminating repair contract, is not met by the native seam rule, because at a
mismatch of one the rule either leaves the seam alone or swaps the odd unit
across it, so repairs on the binary shell can continue indefinitely without
lowering any measure [@oph; @mueller2026agreement]. OPH's Lean development proves
the point on a small carrier (the theorem `demoCarrier_not_confluent`), and
states that its consensus theorems hold for its integer and transactional repair
contracts rather than for the native law. The contribution here is to make the
obstruction quantitative on the carrier itself. Registered as
`pr01-obstruction`.

::: {#prop:census .proposition}
Over the {{SHELL_TOTAL_TWO}} configurations of $\Sigma_2$, the
{{CARRIER_SEAMS}} seams and the two coins, the native rule has
{{SHELL_TRANSITIONS}} transitions. Of these, {{SHELL_STRICT_DESCENT}} strictly
lower [@eq:energy], {{SHELL_ZERO_DESCENT}} leave it unchanged, and
{{SHELL_NOOP}} leave the configuration unchanged. In particular the
zero-descent transitions are not rare: they outnumber the strict descents, and
the multiplicities of the native transitions take exactly the values
{{SHELL_MULTIPLICITIES}}.
:::

The mechanism is [@eq:repair] itself, and the energy identity is [@prop:descent]. At $\lvert d\rvert = 1$ the parity correction absorbs
the whole of the move: coin zero's current is {{PR01_COIN_ZERO_CURRENT}} and coin
one's is {{PR01_COIN_ONE_CURRENT}}, so one coin performs a swap at constant energy
and the other does
nothing at all. The probe reports both currents and both energy decreases, which
are {{PR01_COIN_ZERO_DECREASE}} and {{PR01_COIN_ONE_DECREASE}}. [@fig:descent] shows the census and an excursion.

![The descent census and a mismatch-one excursion. Left: the
{{SHELL_TRANSITIONS}} transitions of the shell, partitioned into
{{SHELL_STRICT_DESCENT}} strict descents, {{SHELL_ZERO_DESCENT}} zero descents and
{{SHELL_NOOP}} no-ops. Right: a one-unit excursion around the carrier through

from `core/controls.py::gated_descent_census`.](../output/figures/fig_descent.png){#fig:descent width=100%}

The consequence is not that the rule fails to converge. It does converge on the
configurations where every broken seam has $\lvert d\rvert \ge 2$, and the strict descents
are exactly those moves. The consequence is that convergence is not *forced*: a
sequence of accepted repairs can cycle on the mismatch-one shell forever, and a
terminating sequence has no normal-form uniqueness. Any theorem that identifies
OPH's repairs with a descent on a potential must therefore either restrict to the
$\lvert d\rvert \ge 2$ moves or add a termination certificate that the native rule does not
supply. The transactional and integer contracts that OPH's consensus theorems do
cover are exactly such enrichments.

## "Free energy" names at least two functionals {#sec:collision}

The second obstruction is terminological but not merely terminological. In the
linear-Gaussian model the interface energy minimised over the interior is
{{FE_INTERFACE}} and the variational free energy at the posterior is
{{FE_VARIATIONAL_AT_POSTERIOR}}. The difference is {{FE_DIFFERENCE}}, which is exactly the
model constant reported by the probe — not a leftover, and not zero. The probe
computes the variational free energy twice, through the surprisal and directly as
energy minus entropy, and both routes give the same constant: the
Gaussian-integral prefactor $\tfrac{1}{2}\log\det L_{xx} - \tfrac{d_x}{2}\log 2\pi$
of the unnormalised density. Registered as `free-energy-name-collision`.

The identification is therefore exact up to a constant that the model fixes and
the vocabulary does not. A comparison of the two programmes by name will
therefore be right about first variations and wrong about levels, and the error is
proportional to the log-normalising constant, which changes whenever the model
does. This is why [@sec:dictionary] assigns `exact-on-domain` rather than `exact`,
and why the registry carries the collision as a disagreement rather than folding
it into the dictionary.

## A stationary blanket does not bound the dynamics {#sec:stationary}

::: {#prop:stationary .proposition}
Order the state as exterior $\eta$, blanket $b$ and interior $\mu$, let the
precision be
$H = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1&0\\1&2&1\\0&1&2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, so that
$H_{\mu\eta} = 0$, and let $Q$ be antisymmetric with $Q_{\mu\eta} = q$. The
diffusion $\mathrm{d}z = -(I - Q)Hz\,\mathrm{d}t + \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{d}W$ has
stationary law $\mathcal{N}(0, H^{-1})$ for every $q$, which satisfies
[@eq:blanket] exactly. Its interior drift is
$\dot\mu = 2q\,\eta + (q-1)\,b - 2\mu$, which at $q = {{BLANKET_Q}}$ has
coefficients {{BLANKET_DRIFT}} on $(\eta, b, \mu)$, so two states with equal
blanket and interior values move differently whenever their exteriors differ,
with cross-drift difference {{BLANKET_CROSS_DRIFT}}.
:::

The stationary law factorises; the dynamics does not. The conditional
independence of [@eq:blanket] is a property of a distribution, and a system can
have it at stationarity while every trajectory through the state space mixes the
interior and the exterior. The probe reports the factorisation and the coupling in
the same mapping, so the reader cannot take the first without also seeing the
second.

This is the disagreement that the critical literature presses
[@bruineberg2022emperor; @biehl2021technical; @aguilera2022particular], and the
probe says it precisely:
the blanket is real as a statistical statement and insufficient as a dynamical
one. A synthesis that needs a boundary to *do* something — to shield, to persist,
to constitute an agent — needs an additional condition on the drift, and that
condition is not implied by [@eq:blanket].

## Stationary blankets leave entropy production free {#sec:solenoid}

::: {#prop:solenoid .proposition}
For $\mathrm{d}x = -(\Gamma - Q)Hx\,\mathrm{d}t + \sqrt{2\Gamma}\,\mathrm{d}W$ with
$\Gamma, H$ positive definite and $Q$ antisymmetric, the law
$\mathcal{N}(0, H^{-1})$ is stationary for every $Q$ and the entropy production
rate is $\operatorname{tr}(Q^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}QH)$. In two dimensions with
$\Gamma = H = I$ and $Q = q\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
the drift has eigenvalues $-1 \pm iq$ and the entropy production is $2q^{2}$:
{{SOLENOIDAL_ENTROPY_PRODUCTION}} at $q \in {{SOLENOIDAL_Q_GRID}}$. Stationarity therefore does not fix
the entropy production, and cannot be used to infer it.
:::

The solenoidal component of the drift — the part that circulates without
changing the stationary density — is invisible to the stationary law and fully
visible in the entropy production. [@fig:blanket] draws both panels.

![Stationary blankets and their freedom. Left: the three drift components at
$q = {{BLANKET_Q}}$, showing that the drift couples the exterior and the interior even
though the stationary law factorises. Right: entropy production against the
solenoidal parameter $q$, which is $2q^{2}$ and therefore free, while every member
of the family is stationary. Generated by `scripts/make_figures.py` from
`core/controls.py::blanket_dynamics_check` and `solenoidal_check`.](../output/figures/fig_blanket.png){#fig:blanket width=100%}

This is the sharpest of the disagreements, because it is a statement about
what a stationary-blanket argument can and cannot conclude. The dissipative
structure is constrained; the circulating structure is not
[@ao2004potential; @seifert2012stochastic; @godreche2019characterising]. A free energy argument that works only
from stationarity therefore cannot reach the thermodynamic quantities that the
solenoidal part controls. Registered as `solenoidal-freedom`.

## Stability is not generic in the coupling {#sec:dstability}

::: {#def:dstability .definition}
A real matrix $A$ is **stable** when every eigenvalue has negative real part, and
**D-stable** when $DA$ is stable for every positive diagonal matrix $D$
[@arrow1958dynamic]. A positive diagonal $D$ is a choice of local repair gains
that leaves the rest point $DAx = 0$ where it was.
:::

::: {#prop:dstability .proposition}
Let $A = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&2&0\\2&-1&3\\0&-3&-1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
be the stable three-node control matrix and
$D_k = \operatorname{diag}(k, 1, 1)$. The
family $D_k A$ has characteristic polynomial
$z^{3} + (k+2)z^{2} + (10-2k)z + 6k$, and its Routh first column is

$$
\Bigl(1,\ \ k+2,\ \ \frac{2\,(10-k^{2})}{k+2},\ \ 6k\Bigr),
$$ {#eq:routh}

so for $k > 0$ every root has negative real part exactly when $k < \sqrt{10}
\approx {{D_STABILITY_ROUTH_ONSET}}$. At $k = 4$ the column is
`{{D_STABILITY_ROUTH_K4}}`, whose two sign changes certify
{{D_STABILITY_RIGHT_HALF_PLANE_COUNT}} right-half-plane roots. $A$ is stable and
not D-stable.
:::

The eigenvalues at $k = 4$ are {{D_STABILITY_REAL_STABLE}} and {{D_STABILITY_REAL_UNSTABLE}}
with imaginary part $\pm {{D_STABILITY_IMAG_UNSTABLE}}$: the complex pair has positive real
part, and the system spirals out.

What is lost is a free-energy form. A linear law admits the Helmholtz
decomposition $A = -(\Gamma - Q)H$ with $\Gamma, H$ positive definite and $Q$
antisymmetric -- the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck form whose stationary density is
$\propto \exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}x^{\top}Hx)$ -- exactly when $A$ is stable: with
$\Gamma = I$ the Lyapunov equation $AP + PA^{\top} = -2I$ gives $H = P^{-1}$ and
$Q = \tfrac{1}{2}(AP - PA^{\top})$ [@khalil2002nonlinear]. For the unscaled $A$
the source's certificate is exact: $P$ is positive definite with
$\det P = {{D_STABILITY_LYAPUNOV_DETERMINANT}}$, the residual of the Lyapunov
equation is {{D_STABILITY_LYAPUNOV_RESIDUAL}}, and $Q$ is antisymmetric
[@mueller2026agreement]. At $k = 4$ no positive-definite solution exists, because
$D_4A$ is unstable, so one positive local gain removes the form without moving
the rest. The control is not an artefact of linear algebra: with
$W = 4(A + I)$ and a logistic activation, the bounded neural field
$f(v) = -v + W\sigma(v) + b$ with $b = -W(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})^{\top}$
has $f(0) = 0$ and Jacobian exactly $A$ at the origin, which the probe confirms
with zero error. The obstruction is therefore not that the algebra fails. It is
that stability is a property of a point in a parameter space, and the
identification of a repair rule with a stable descent needs D-stability
([@def:dstability]), every admissible gain on the stable side. Here the stable region is $k < \sqrt{10}$,
which is not the whole admissible range. Registered as `d-stability`.

$k \in {{D_STABILITY_SAMPLES}}$, with the imaginary axis drawn; the pair that crosses

## Reduction needs a threshold {#sec:mediator}

::: {#prop:mediator .proposition}
For the three-block Gaussian model with mediator coupling $\varepsilon$, a pairwise
representation of the reduced boundary exists exactly when
$\varepsilon \ge {{MEDIATOR_THRESHOLD}}$. Below the threshold the pairwise margin
is $3\varepsilon - 1$, which is {{MEDIATOR_MARGIN_BELOW}} at $\varepsilon = {{MEDIATOR_EPSILON_BELOW}}$; at the
threshold it is {{MEDIATOR_MARGIN_AT}} and the decomposition exists, is positive
semidefinite, and reproduces the target.
:::

![The mediator threshold. Left: the pairwise margin $3\varepsilon-1$ against
$\varepsilon$, {{MEDIATOR_MARGIN_BELOW}} at $\varepsilon = {{MEDIATOR_EPSILON_BELOW}}$ and crossing zero
at the threshold `{{MEDIATOR_THRESHOLD}}`. Right: the
reduction error at and below the threshold, showing that the worst-case error
floor is {{REDUCTION_WORST_CASE}} while retaining the record is exact. Generated by
`scripts/make_figures.py` from `core/controls.py::mediator_pairwise_margin` and
`reduction_control`.](../output/figures/fig_mediator.png){#fig:mediator width=85%}

The threshold is not an artefact of the parametrisation. Necessity is a trace
inequality: each pairwise block must carry the off-diagonal entry
$\pm\tfrac{1}{3}$ of the target, so a positive semidefinite block has diagonal
sum at least $\tfrac{2}{3}$, and the three blocks together need at least two units
of trace against the target's $1 + 3\varepsilon$. Sufficiency is an explicit
decomposition, and at $\varepsilon = {{MEDIATOR_THRESHOLD}}$ every block in it is
rank one: the pairwise form sits on the boundary of the semidefinite cone there,
and below it the cone is left. Two further disagreements belong with
it. The two competing reduction criteria disagree — the same observation scores
{{REDUCTION_SAME_OBSERVATION}}, the first criterion scores {{REDUCTION_FIRST_AFTER}}
afterwards and the second {{REDUCTION_SECOND_AFTER}}, the response distance is
{{REDUCTION_RESPONSE_DISTANCE}}, and the worst-case error has a lower bound of {{REDUCTION_WORST_CASE}}
— so the choice of criterion changes the verdict rather than the precision. And
the iteration schedule is not a detail: the centred contrast after twelve steps
differs between the full and the half-damped schedules by exactly
${{SCHEDULE_AFFINE_CONTRAST}}$ in absolute value, so a claim about "the"
schedule path has to name the schedule [@mueller2026agreement]. Registered as
`mediator-obstruction`, with `reduction-criterion` and `schedule-path` beside it.

## Existence does not imply reachability {#sec:existence}

::: {#prop:existence .proposition}
The two-node relaxation has Jacobian eigenvalues $1 \pm 2\,i$, a relaxation orbit
whose radius stays in $[{{TWO_NODE_RELAXATION_MIN_RADIUS_MEASURED}},\
{{TWO_NODE_RELAXATION_MAX_RADIUS}}]$ with period
${{TWO_NODE_RELAXATION_PERIOD_MEASURED}}$, and a synchronous clipped map whose
orbit minimum radius is ${{TWO_NODE_SYNC_MIN_RADIUS}}$ — a different object with a
different bound.
:::

The source states the bound as ${{TWO_NODE_RELAXATION_MIN_RADIUS}}$ and the period
as ${{TWO_NODE_RELAXATION_PERIOD}}$; the probe measures
${{TWO_NODE_RELAXATION_MIN_RADIUS_MEASURED}}$ and
${{TWO_NODE_RELAXATION_PERIOD_MEASURED}}$. Both numbers are reported, because the
difference between a source's bound and a package's measurement is exactly the
kind of thing a reader needs to see rather than to trust. [@fig:orbit] draws the
orbit.

![Existence without reachability. Left: the relaxation orbit in the plane, with
the annulus $[{{TWO_NODE_RELAXATION_MIN_RADIUS_MEASURED}},
{{TWO_NODE_RELAXATION_MAX_RADIUS}}]$ that contains it; the orbit neither escapes
nor reaches the origin. Right: the radius against time, showing the period
${{TWO_NODE_RELAXATION_PERIOD_MEASURED}}$ and the contrast with the synchronous

`scripts/make_figures.py` from `core/controls.py::two_node_relaxation_orbit`.](../output/figures/fig_orbit.png){#fig:orbit width=100%}

The disagreement is subtle and worth spelling out. Both programmes can prove
that a fixed point exists, and both can compute it; the probe finds the complete
fixed-point set of the clipped map by exact case analysis over its nine clipping
regimes, and it is the origin alone. The synchronous orbit's radius bound is
measured after a transient, which the probe records. The relaxation dynamics has a
Jacobian with positive real part at the fixed point, so the fixed point is
unstable, and the orbit is bounded away from it. Existence and reachability are
different questions, and a synthesis that infers the second from the first is
wrong in a way that no amount of algebraic care will fix. Registered as
`existence-without-reachability`.

## Direct and adjoint gradients differ {#sec:directed}

For a two-dimensional system at a stable fixed point with the non-symmetric
Jacobian $J$ and loss derivative $c$, a forward nudge measures the direct
response $-J^{-1} c = {{DIRECTED_GRADIENT_DIRECT}}$, while the gradient of the
loss with respect to the parameters needs the adjoint
$-J^{-\top} c = {{DIRECTED_GRADIENT_ADJOINT}}$, the recurrent back-propagation of
[@pineda1987generalization]. Only the adjoint is the gradient; the two coincide
exactly when $J$ is symmetric, which is the case an energy-based network
supplies. A learning rule that reads the forward response therefore does not
follow the gradient of a directed network, and the disagreement is not a
numerical artefact. Registered as `directed-gradient`.

## The four-cycle has an incompatible local minimum {#sec:four-cycle}

On the four-cycle with an equality constraint on every edge, the compatible
states are all-zeros and all-ones, with no violations, while the state `0011`
violates two edges. Every single flip of that state also leaves two violations,
so it is a (non-strict) *local* minimum of the violation count sitting on a
plateau, and a descent method that only accepts strictly improving moves cannot
leave it. The probe reports the violations after each of
the four flips, all of which are {{FOUR_CYCLE_VIOLATIONS}}.

This is the mechanism behind the traps that repair-as-descent arguments have to
exclude by hand. It is also a concrete instance of the general point: a descent
guarantee is a statement about a landscape, and a landscape with plateaux has
local minima that are not global ones. Registered as `four-cycle-local-minimum`.

## Twelve vertices is not one solid {#sec:cuboctahedron}

The IVM's first shell and the carrier's vertex set both contain twelve points.
That count is not an identification, and the probe exhibits the difference rather
than the coincidence.

::: {#prop:cuboctahedron .proposition}
The twelve nearest neighbours, joined when their squared distance is eight,
form the cuboctahedron graph: {{CUBOCTAHEDRON_DEGREE}}-regular, ${{CUBOCTAHEDRON_EDGES}}$ edges, eight
triangles and six squares, with tetravolume
${{CUBOCTAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME}}$. The carrier's graph is
{{ICOSAHEDRON_DEGREE}}-regular with ${{CARRIER_SEAMS}}$ edges and twenty triangles, and its
solid has tetravolume ${{ICOSAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME}}$. The two graphs have the same
vertex count and are not isomorphic.
:::

The probe reports both degree sequences, both edge counts, both face
decompositions, both volumes, and the fact that the icosahedral realisation of the
carrier is isomorphic to the *carrier* and not to the neighbour graph. The
tetravolumes settle it without reference to the graph at all:
{{CUBOCTAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME}} against ${{ICOSAHEDRON_TETRAVOLUME}}\ldots$, one integral and one algebraic.
The failure costs more than a footnote, because the two solids support opposite
dynamical readings. The cuboctahedron's neighbours are arranged so that each
vertex sees four others, which gives a lattice with a four-fold local order; the
carrier's ports see five, which is what puts the surd $\sqrt{5}$ into the
spectral gap $(5-\sqrt{5})/60$ [@eq:gamma] and what the FEP's blanket count of
{{GMRF_BLANKET_SIZE}} inherits. A synthesis that identified the two by their
common twelve would import a coordination number the carrier does not have.

Both solids are kissing arrangements of twelve spheres around one, which is
where the confusion starts, and they attain the kissing number in different
ways. With the twelve centres at twice the sphere radius from the centre, the
cuboctahedron's squared edge over squared circumradius is
{{KISSING_CUBOCTAHEDRON_EDGE_RATIO_SQUARED}}, so every sphere touches four of its
neighbours as well as the centre: the rigid arrangement of the densest packing.
The icosahedron's ratio is $2 - 2\sqrt{5}/5 \approx
{{KISSING_ICOSAHEDRON_EDGE_RATIO_SQUARED}}$, so its spheres touch the centre and
none of each other. The slack is what Fuller's jitterbug uses to carry the
vector equilibrium continuously through the icosahedron to the octahedron
[@fuller1975synergetics; @verheyen1989jitterbug], and the volume is not
conserved along the way. Synergetics therefore does not conflate the two solids
-- it distinguishes them and connects them by a motion -- and the obstruction is
to a reader who identifies them by their count.

The same coincidence is why this entry is a disagreement rather than a
complementarity: the neighbour *cell* of [@sec:packing] is the rhombic
dodecahedron, of tetravolume six, whose twelve faces face the twelve neighbours
and whose graph has six four-valent and eight three-valent vertices. Three
distinct solids in this geometry have twelve-fold features, and only one of them
is the carrier. Registered as `cuboctahedron-vs-icosahedron`.

## The shell index and the free energy determine neither each other {#sec:shell-energy}

::: {#prop:shell-energy .proposition}
Let $F(z) = \tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z\rVert^{2}$ be the equal-covariance divergence of
[@prop:kl] at a lattice site $z$, and $k(z)$ its coordination-shell index
([@def:coordination]). Neither is a function of the other. The first five shells
carry {{SHELL_FREE_ENERGY_LEVEL_COUNTS}} distinct values of $F$, and the levels
{{SHELL_SHARED_FREE_ENERGY_LEVELS}} are each carried by two shells. On shell $k$
the divergence ranges from $2k^{2}$ (from $2k^{2}+2$ when $k$ is odd) up to
$4k^{2}$, and reaches $4k^{2}$ only at the twelve vertices $k\,m$.
:::

The probe walks the shells, collects the distinct values of $F$ on each, reports
the counts per shell, the levels that appear on more than one shell, and the
extreme values of $F$ on every shell through {{SHELL_GAUGE_CHECKED_THROUGH}}. A
shell is not a level set of $F$, and a level of $F$ is not confined to one shell.
The lower bound does give one inequality: $F \le L$ forces $k \le \sqrt{L/2}$, so a
bounded free energy bounds the shell index from above. What fails is the
identification, in both directions.

The reason is exact and easy to state once the shell has its closed form. The
shell index is the gauge $g(u) = \max(\lVert u\rVert_{\infty},
\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert_{1})$ of [@eq:shell], a polyhedral norm whose unit ball
is the vector equilibrium, while $F$ is a Euclidean quadratic form whose level
sets are spheres. A sphere and a cuboctahedron touch only along the
cuboctahedron's twelve vertex directions, which is why $F$ attains its
shell maximum only at $k\,m$ and why the face points of shell $k$ sit closer to the
centre than its vertices, by up to a factor of $\sqrt{2}$ in distance.

[@fig:shell-gauge] draws both halves of the statement.

![The coordination shell against the free energy. Left: the points of the face-centred cubic lattice $D_3$ in the plane $u_3 = 0$, coloured by the closed-form shell index $g$ of [@eq:shell]; in this slice the shells are nested squares, and the overlaid circles are levels of the free energy, which cross several shells. Right: for every shell through {{SHELL_GAUGE_CHECKED_THROUGH}}, the range of $F$ over the shell as a bar and each distinct level as a dot, with the levels shared between shells highlighted and the curves $2k^{2}$ and $4k^{2}$ drawn; the maximum is attained only at the twelve vertices of each shell. Generated by `scripts/make_figures.py` from `geometry/lattice.py`.](../output/figures/fig_shell_gauge.png){#fig:shell-gauge width=100%}

This is the paper's only *coordinate-level* obstruction. It does not say that the
FEP's functional is wrong or that Synergetics' metric is wrong; it says that a
composite vocabulary that treats "shell" and "free energy" as two names for the
same stratification will be inconsistent at the first shell with more than one
radius, which is shell two. Registered as `shell-vs-free-energy`.

## The shells around a port are not the lattice's shells {#sec:vertex-shells}

::: {#prop:vertex-shells .proposition}
The ports at graph distance one, two and three from a port number
{{CARRIER_SHELL_SIZES}}, the graph is distance-regular with intersection array
{{CARRIER_INTERSECTION_ARRAY}} and diameter {{CARRIER_DIAMETER}}, and the
lattice's own shells at the same three radii number {{LATTICE_SHELL_SIZES}}.
:::

The two sequences {{CARRIER_SHELL_SIZES}} and {{LATTICE_SHELL_SIZES}} disagree
from the first term: the carrier's first shell is a port's
{{CARRIER_DEGREE}} neighbours because the icosahedron is five-valent, while the
IVM's first shell holds {{IVM_NEIGHBOR_MOVES}} sites because the lattice is
twelve-coordinated by construction. The proposal that a
port's neighbourhood is a lattice shell therefore fails on the smallest possible
test, and no choice of radius repairs it: the carrier is finite with diameter
{{CARRIER_DIAMETER}}, so its shells stop, while the lattice's do not.

What survives is a structural statement rather than an identification. The
carrier is distance-regular, with intersection array
{{CARRIER_INTERSECTION_ARRAY}} read off a single representative per shell, so
its neighbourhood structure is rigid: from any port, at any distance, the
outward and backward degrees are the same [@brouwer1989distance]. That is a
stronger fact than the identification it replaces, and it follows from the
symmetry: the group of order {{SYMMETRY_GROUP_ORDER}} is transitive on the pairs
of ports at each distance, and a distance-transitive graph is distance-regular.

## `Twelve around one` names a family, not an object {#sec:families}

::: {#prop:families .proposition}
The phrase covers at least two solids with different counting invariants. The
icosahedron is five-valent with {{CARRIER_TREE_NUMBER}} spanning trees; the
cuboctahedron is four-valent, carries {{CUBOCTAHEDRON_TRIANGLES}} triangles and
{{CUBOCTAHEDRON_SQUARES}} squares, {{CUBOCTAHEDRON_MATCHINGS}} perfect matchings,
and {{CUBOCTAHEDRON_SPANNING_TREES}} spanning trees, which is
{{CUBOCTAHEDRON_SPANNING_TREES_ROOT}} squared.
:::

This is the same failure [@sec:cuboctahedron] reports, one level down. There the
shared quantity was the vertex count; here it is the phrase itself. A reader who
is told that a structure is "twelve around one" has been told the coordination
number and nothing else, and the coordination number does not determine the
object: the two solids differ in degree, in triangle count, in matching count,
and in the spanning-tree number, whose ratio is exactly {{FAMILIES_TREE_RATIO}}.

The counting invariant is what does the work. Spanning trees are computed by the
same determinant ([@eq:tree]; [@prop:tree]) in both cases, on adjacency matrices derived independently --
the carrier's from OPH's oriented base faces, the cuboctahedron's from the
neighbouring lattice sites -- so the numbers are comparable even though the
objects are not the same. What the registry records is that a claim of the form
"the carrier is the twelve-around-one solid" is under-determined, and that the
remedy is a refinement rather than a tolerance. Registered as
`twelve-around-one-families`.

## What the obstructions have in common

The obstructions fall into three kinds. The first is a *plateau*: zero-descent
moves (PR-01), plateaux in the violation landscape (the four-cycle), and
constant-energy swaps all describe dynamics that cannot be ordered into a
descent. The second is a *missing invariant*: the entropy production of a
stationary blanket, the stability of a coupling outside its stable region, and
the direction of a gradient all vary within a class that a coarser vocabulary
treats as fixed.

The third is the one a correspondence registry is best placed to catch: *an
invariant too coarse to identify the object it is asked to identify*. Twelve
vertices do not determine a solid ([@prop:cuboctahedron]), the phrase "twelve
around one" covers two solids it does not separate ([@prop:families]), a port's
neighbourhood is not a lattice shell ([@prop:vertex-shells]), and a shell index
does not determine a free energy ([@prop:shell-energy]). None of these failed
because a number disagreed; each failed because the shared quantity was never an
identification, and a table of matched counts would have reported each as a
success.

A synthesis that wants to identify the three programmes must therefore add
structure that excludes plateaux, fixes the missing invariants, and refines the
coarse ones. That is a research programme, not a corollary, and the next section
reports what each programme can contribute to it.
