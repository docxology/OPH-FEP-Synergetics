# Agreement: the eight surviving identifications {#sec:correspondences}

The registry records {{REGISTRY_ISOMORPHISMS}} isomorphisms. This section states
each one, gives the exact values the probe reports, and says what the
identification does *not* cover. [@fig:registry] shows the whole registry at a
glance.

## The port chart is a consequence, not a transcription {#sec:port-chart}

[@prop:carrier] is exact in the strongest sense available: the derivation returns
{{CARRIER_PORTS}} ports, {{CARRIER_SEAMS}} seams and {{CARRIER_FACES}} faces; every
degree is $5$; the twenty triangles of the derived graph are exactly the twenty
oriented faces; and the derived adjacency equals the declared adjacency. The
probe reports all six facts separately, so a failure of any one of them is
visible rather than masked by the conjunction.

This matters for a reason that is methodological rather than mathematical. A
published table of adjacencies can be copied with a transposition and still
produce a plausible graph. Deriving the graph from the faces, and then checking
that its triangles reproduce the faces, closes the loop: a transposition
anywhere in the source data would break the round trip. The same pattern is used
wherever this package reads a table that has consequences.

## The spectrum is closed-form {#sec:spectrum}

[@prop:spectrum] gives the full spectrum. The probe reports the eigenvalues as
`{{CARRIER_LAMBDA1}}` three times, $6$ five times, and $5+\sqrt{5} \approx
7.236$ three times, with the zero eigenvalue once, and checks that the Laplacian
really is $5I - A$ rather than a normalised variant.

The gap is the quantity that carries dynamical meaning. Normalising by the total
degree,

$$
\gamma \;=\; \frac{\lambda_1}{2\lvert E\rvert} \;=\; \frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{60} \;=\; {{CARRIER_SHELL_GAP}},
$$ {#eq:gamma}

gives a number that is invariant under rescaling and is the one that appears in
the relaxation estimates below. The corresponding **repair chain** is the
symmetric stochastic matrix

$$
P \;=\; I - \frac{L}{2\lvert E\rvert},
$$ {#eq:chain}

whose rows sum to one, whose entries are non-negative because every degree is at
most $2\lvert E\rvert$, and whose second eigenvalue is $1-\gamma = 0.9539344663$ — the
`certificate-species` correspondence, reported by the probe as a row-stochastic,
symmetric, non-negative matrix with exactly that second eigenvalue and induced
infinity norm $1$. The chain is the natural object behind the phrase "the shell
mixes at the spectral gap": its mixing is governed by $\gamma$, and $\gamma$ is a
property of the carrier alone.

## The shell is counted exactly {#sec:shell}

[@def:shell] gives the count. The probe reports
$\lvert\Sigma_2\rvert = \binom{13}{11} = {{SHELL_TOTAL_TWO}}$ total-two configurations and
{{SHELL_BINARY}} binary ones, and cross-checks both numbers against the repair
module, which computes them independently. Two independent computations of the
same integer is a weak check in general; here it is worth having, because the
binary count is the one that the repair census below partitions.

## The native repair law is reproduced, not approximated {#sec:repair-law}

The `native-repair-law` correspondence reimplements OPH's rule on exact integers
and checks it over all {{SHELL_TRANSITIONS}} transitions of the
{{SHELL_TOTAL_TWO}}-state shell. The rule itself is [@eq:repair]; the closed form
for the energy drop is $\Delta E = -\bigl(d^{2} - (\lvert d\rvert \bmod 2)\bigr)/4$, reported
by the probe as a string so that the formula, and not merely a sample of its
values, is part of the record.

The reimplementation is exact in the sense that matters: it is not a numerical
approximation of OPH's rule but the same integer arithmetic, and the energy
identity is checked rather than assumed. What the correspondence does *not* say
is that OPH's rule is the right dynamics; that is an empirical question, and
OPH's own status document records that no retained laboratory comparison selects
a native record law [@oph].

::: {#prop:ve .proposition}
Let $L$ be the carrier Laplacian partitioned into recorded ports $o$ and free
ports $x$, and let
$x^{*}(o) = -L_{xx}^{-1} L_{xo}\, o$ be the harmonic extension of the records.
Then the variational free energy of [@eq:gaussian] is minimised at
$x^{*}(o)$, with

$$
S \;=\; L_{oo} - L_{ox} L_{xx}^{-1} L_{xo},
\qquad
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{G}}(o) - \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{G}}(o_0)
\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(o^{\top} S\, o - o_0^{\top} S\, o_0\bigr)
\;+\; \rho(o) - \rho(o_0).
$$ {#eq:ve}
:::

The probe reports that the fixed-point iteration `perceive` reaches the harmonic
extension in $51$ iterations with residual $7.4\times 10^{-11}$, that the
posterior mean equals the harmonic extension, and that the free energy at the
posterior exceeds the energy at the minimiser by $0.0$ to machine precision. The
excess free energy at the posterior is exactly zero because the bound is tight
there: [@eq:vfe] is an equality when $q$ is the posterior.

This is the paper's central positive result about the FEP, and its scope should
be stated as carefully as the result. [@prop:ve] holds in the linear-Gaussian
corner, where the posterior is Gaussian, the free energy is a quadratic form, and
the harmonic extension is available in closed form. It does not assert that a
non-Gaussian model has a quadratic free energy, and it does not assert that the
iteration converges for a general graph; the residual is reported so that a
failure of convergence would be visible as a number rather than as a hang.

## The free-energy dictionary has a precise scope {#sec:dictionary}

Three quantities are routinely called "surprisal", and the source literature
distinguishes five: {{PAPER_FIVE_SURPRISES}} [@mueller2026agreement]. In the linear-Gaussian model this
package computes three of them, and [@tbl:surprises] reports what happens.

| Quantity | Definition | Value in the probe model |
|:---------|:-----------|:-------------------------|
| numerical free energy | $\tfrac{1}{2} r^{\top}\Pi\, r$ at the residual $r$ | $1.118034$ |
| Bayesian surprise | $\tfrac{1}{2} g^{\top} L^{-1} g$ | $0.25$ |
| surprisal | $-\log p(o)$ up to the model constant | $-0.913571$ |
| variational free energy | [@eq:gaussian] at the posterior | $-0.913571$ |

: The three surprises in the linear-Gaussian corner, with the values the probe
reports. The numerical and Bayesian quantities differ from the surprisal by the
model constant; the sandwich bound brackets the Bayesian value. {#tbl:surprises}

The probe reports a sandwich with lower bound $0.208333$ and upper bound
$0.625$, and the Bayesian value $0.25$ lies inside it. It reports that the
variational free energy and the surprisal agree to machine precision, because at
the posterior the bound is tight. And it reports that the interface energy
minimised over the interior is $0.041667$ while the variational free energy is
$-1.246904$, a difference of $-1.288571$ which is exactly the model constant.

The status is `exact-on-domain` rather than `exact` for that reason: the
identification is exact as a statement about *first variation*, and the levels
differ by a constant that the model fixes but the identification does not. A
reader who takes "free energy" to name a single functional will draw wrong
conclusions at the first model change; the constant is the visible trace of that
risk.

## A sheaf computes the same Laplacian {#sec:sheaf}

::: {#prop:sheaf .proposition}
Give every port the stalk $\mathbb{R}$ and every seam the identity restriction.
The resulting cellular sheaf on $K$ has {{CARRIER_SEAMS}} edges,
Laplacian equal to the carrier Laplacian, one-dimensional space of global
sections (the constants), and injective record maps at every port.
:::

The probe reports all of this, and it reports two negative controls: a chain
sheaf with one record has a one-dimensional space of global sections, and a chain
sheaf with no record has none. The controls matter because a sheaf whose global
sections are one-dimensional for every choice of records would not be saying
anything about the carrier.

The sheaf formulation is the one that generalises. A scalar identity sheaf is
the simplest case; the same construction with non-identity restrictions is how
the FEP's precision structure would enter, and the harmonic-extension identity of
[@prop:ve] is the same computation written in sheaf language
[@hansen2019sheaves; @robinson2014topological]. The agreement reported here is
therefore not a coincidence of two implementations but a statement that the
carrier's Laplacian is the coboundary Laplacian of a sheaf, which is the bridge
[@proposal2026] proposed.

## What the eight agreements do not say

None of the eight claims identifies OPH's *dynamics* with the FEP's *functional*.
The agreements are about the carrier, its spectrum, its counts, its repair rule as
an integer map, the Gaussian free energy on it, the dictionary in the Gaussian
corner, and the sheaf whose Laplacian it is. Every one of those is a statement
about a finite object that both programmes can be given.

The next section reports the eleven places where the identification fails, and
most of them fail for one reason: the authors of each programme attach a
*dynamical* claim to that shared object, and the dynamical claims do not agree.
