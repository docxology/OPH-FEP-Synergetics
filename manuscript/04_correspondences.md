## The port chart is a consequence, not a transcription {#sec:port-chart}

[@prop:carrier] is exact in the strongest sense available: the derivation returns
{{CARRIER_PORTS}} ports, {{CARRIER_SEAMS}} seams and {{CARRIER_FACES}} faces; every
degree is {{CARRIER_DEGREE}}; the twenty triangles of the derived graph are exactly the twenty
oriented faces; and the derived adjacency equals the declared adjacency. The
probe reports all six facts separately, so a failure of any one of them is
visible rather than masked by the conjunction.

This matters for a reason that is methodological rather than mathematical. A
published table of adjacencies can be copied with a transposition and still
produce a plausible graph. Deriving the graph from the faces, and then checking
that its triangles reproduce the faces, closes the loop: a transposition
anywhere in the source data would break the round trip. The same pattern is used
wherever this package reads a table that has consequences.

## The spectrum is closed-form {#sec:spectrum}

[@prop:spectrum] gives the full spectrum. The probe reports the eigenvalues as
`{{CARRIER_LAMBDA1}}` three times, six five times, and
$5+\sqrt{5} \approx {{SPECTRAL_LAMBDA_MAX}}$ three times, with the zero eigenvalue once, and checks that the Laplacian
really is $5I - A$ rather than a normalised variant.

The gap is the quantity that carries dynamical meaning. Normalising by the total
degree,

$$
\gamma \;=\; \frac{\lambda_1}{2\lvert E\rvert} \;=\; \frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{60} \;=\; {{CARRIER_SHELL_GAP}},
$$ {#eq:gamma}

gives a number that is invariant under rescaling and is the one that appears in
the relaxation estimates below. The corresponding **repair chain** is the
symmetric stochastic matrix

$$
P \;=\; I - \frac{L}{2\lvert E\rvert},
$$ {#eq:chain}

whose rows sum to one, whose entries are non-negative because every degree is at
most $2\lvert E\rvert$, and whose second eigenvalue is $1-\gamma = {{CERTIFICATE_SECOND_EIGENVALUE}}$ — the
`certificate-species` correspondence, reported by the probe as a row-stochastic,
symmetric, non-negative matrix with exactly that second eigenvalue and induced
infinity norm {{CERTIFICATE_INFINITY_NORM}}. The chain of [@eq:chain] is the natural object behind the phrase "the shell
mixes at the spectral gap": its mixing is governed by $\gamma$, and $\gamma$ is a
property of the carrier alone.

## The shell is counted exactly {#sec:shell}

[@def:shell] gives the count. The probe reports
$\lvert\Sigma_2\rvert = \binom{13}{11} = {{SHELL_TOTAL_TWO}}$ total-two configurations and
{{SHELL_BINARY}} binary ones, and cross-checks both numbers against the repair
module, which computes them independently. Two independent computations of the
same integer is a weak check in general; here it is worth having, because the
binary count is the one that the repair census below partitions.

## The native repair law is reproduced, not approximated {#sec:repair-law}

The `native-repair-law` correspondence reimplements OPH's rule on exact integers
and checks it over all {{SHELL_TRANSITIONS}} transitions of the
{{SHELL_TOTAL_TWO}}-state shell. The rule itself is [@eq:repair]; the closed form
for the energy drop is $\Delta E = -\bigl(d^{2} - (\lvert d\rvert \bmod 2)\bigr)/4$, reported
by the probe as a string so that the formula, and not merely a sample of its
values, is part of the record.

The reimplementation is exact in the sense that matters: it is not a numerical
approximation of OPH's rule but the same integer arithmetic, and the energy
identity is checked rather than assumed. What the correspondence does *not* say
is that OPH's rule is the right dynamics; that is an empirical question, and
OPH's own status document records that no retained laboratory comparison selects
a native record law [@oph].

::: {#prop:ve .proposition}
Let $L$ be the carrier Laplacian partitioned into recorded ports $o$ and free
ports $x$, and let
$x^{*}(o) = -L_{xx}^{-1} L_{xo}\, o$ be the harmonic extension of the records.
Then the variational free energy of [@eq:gaussian] is minimised at
$x^{*}(o)$, with

$$
S \;=\; L_{oo} - L_{ox} L_{xx}^{-1} L_{xo},
\qquad
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{G}}(o) - \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{G}}(o_0)
\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(o^{\top} S\, o - o_0^{\top} S\, o_0\bigr)
\;+\; \rho(o) - \rho(o_0).
$$ {#eq:ve}
:::

The probe reports that, under [@eq:ve], the fixed-point iteration `perceive`
reaches the harmonic extension in {{VE_ITERATIONS}} iterations with residual
{{VE_RESIDUAL}}, that the posterior mean equals the harmonic extension, and that
the free energy at the posterior exceeds the energy at the minimiser by
{{VE_EXCESS}} to machine precision. The
excess free energy at the posterior is exactly zero because the bound is tight
there: [@eq:vfe] is an equality when $q$ is the posterior.

This is the paper's central positive result about the FEP, and its scope should
be stated as carefully as the result. [@prop:ve] holds in the linear-Gaussian
corner, where the posterior is Gaussian, the free energy is a quadratic form, and
the harmonic extension is available in closed form. It does not assert that a
non-Gaussian model has a quadratic free energy, and it does not assert that the
iteration converges for a general graph; the residual is reported so that a
failure of convergence would be visible as a number rather than as a hang.

## The free-energy dictionary has a precise scope {#sec:dictionary}

Three quantities are routinely called "surprisal", and the source literature
distinguishes five: {{PAPER_FIVE_SURPRISES}} [@mueller2026agreement]. In the linear-Gaussian model this
package computes three of them, and [@tbl:surprises] reports what happens. The
registry entry is `free-energy-dictionary`.

| Quantity | Definition | Value in the probe model |
|:---------|:-----------|:-------------------------|
| numerical free energy | $\tfrac{1}{2} r^{\top}\Pi\, r$ at the residual $r$ | {{FE_NUMERICAL}} |
| Bayesian surprise | $\tfrac{1}{2} g^{\top} L^{-1} g$ | {{FE_BAYESIAN}} |
| surprisal | $-\log p(o)$ up to the model constant | {{FE_SURPRISAL}} |
| variational free energy | [@eq:gaussian] at the posterior | {{FE_VARIATIONAL}} |

: The three surprises in the linear-Gaussian corner, with the values the probe
reports. The numerical and Bayesian quantities differ from the surprisal by the
model constant; the sandwich bound brackets the Bayesian value. {#tbl:surprises}

The probe reports a sandwich with lower bound {{FE_SANDWICH_LOWER}} and upper
bound {{FE_SANDWICH_UPPER}}, and the Bayesian value {{FE_BAYESIAN}} lies inside it. It reports that the
variational free energy and the surprisal agree to machine precision, because at
the posterior the bound is tight. And it reports that the interface energy
minimised over the interior is {{FE_INTERFACE}} while the variational free energy
is {{FE_VARIATIONAL_AT_POSTERIOR}}, a difference of {{FE_DIFFERENCE}} which is exactly the
model constant {{FE_MODEL_CONSTANT}}.

The status is `exact-on-domain` rather than `exact` for that reason: the
identification is exact as a statement about *first variation*, and the levels
differ by a constant that the model fixes but the identification does not. A
reader who takes "free energy" to name a single functional will draw wrong
conclusions at the first model change; the constant is the visible trace of that
risk.

## A sheaf computes the same Laplacian {#sec:sheaf}

::: {#prop:sheaf .proposition}
Give every port the stalk $\mathbb{R}$ and every seam the identity restriction.
The resulting cellular sheaf on $K$ has {{CARRIER_SEAMS}} edges,
Laplacian equal to the carrier Laplacian, one-dimensional space of global
sections (the constants), and injective record maps at every port.
:::

The probe reports all of this, and it reports two negative controls: a chain
sheaf with one record has a one-dimensional space of global sections, and a chain
sheaf with no record has none. The controls matter because a sheaf whose global
sections are one-dimensional for every choice of records would not be saying
anything about the carrier. Registered as `sheaf-agreement`.

The sheaf formulation is the one that generalises. A scalar identity sheaf is
the simplest case; the same construction with non-identity restrictions is how
the FEP's precision structure would enter, and the harmonic-extension identity of
[@prop:ve] is the same computation written in sheaf language
[@hansen2019sheaves; @robinson2014topological]. The agreement reported here is
therefore not a coincidence of two implementations but a statement that the
carrier's Laplacian is the coboundary Laplacian of a sheaf, which is the bridge
[@proposal2026] proposed.

## The carrier counts its own spanning trees {#sec:tree-number}

::: {#prop:tree .proposition}
Delete any row and the matching column of the carrier Laplacian. The determinant
of the remaining $11 \times 11$ matrix is an integer, and that integer is the
number of spanning trees of the seam graph:

$$
\tau(K) \;=\; {{CARRIER_TREE_NUMBER}} \;=\; \frac{1}{12}\prod_{i=2}^{12}\lambda_i .
$$ {#eq:tree}
:::

The probe reports the determinant computed in exact rational arithmetic, and,
separately, the product of the eleven nonzero eigenvalues computed in floating
point: the first is {{CARRIER_TREE_NUMBER}}, the second agrees with it to nine
digits. The two computations are not redundant. The eigenvalues include the surds
$5 \pm \sqrt{5}$ three times each, so an exact integer arising from a product of
irrationals is a statement about the arithmetic and not about the sampling; the
floating-point product is reported next to it because the disagreement between
the two is the honest error bar on the spectral route.

The count is not decoration. It says that the carrier's acyclic seam sets are a
finite, exactly known family, so any claim about the existence of an agreement
configuration can be phrased as a claim about a member of a set of known size.
[@sec:existence] is the obstruction that shows why the size of that set does not
by itself settle reachability.

## The Gaussian reading puts the independence exactly on the seams {#sec:gmrf}

Read $L$ as the precision of a Gaussian field on the ports, in the canonical
gauge where the field is centred by its mean. Then conditional independence is a
property of the precision's zeros: two ports are independent given the rest
exactly when their precision entry vanishes, which here happens exactly when they
are not joined by a seam. The probe reports the independence pattern as a
${{CARRIER_PORTS}} \times {{CARRIER_PORTS}}$ matrix over all {{GMRF_ORDERED_PAIRS}} ordered pairs of distinct ports, checks that
it equals the adjacency pattern, and reports the three quantities that make the
reading usable.

Every seam's pairwise precision is {{GMRF_PAIRWISE_PRECISION}}, the Markov
blanket of every port is its {{GMRF_BLANKET_SIZE}} neighbours, and the exact
marginal variance of a port is {{GMRF_MARGINAL_VARIANCE}}, which is {{GMRF_MARGINAL_VARIANCE_EXACT}}. The
variance is computed as the reciprocal spectrum sum
$\sum_{i\ge 2} 1/\lambda_i$ in exact arithmetic and cross-checked against the
pseudo-inverse of $L$, so the number is an identity rather than a fitted value.

Two cautions belong with this correspondence, and the paper states them here
because the FEP literature uses the same words for weaker claims. The
independence is *conditional* on every other port; it is not marginal
independence, and it is not a statement that the blanket cuts the dynamics. The
second caution is the subject of [@sec:stationary].

## Two cohomology dimensions, one global section {#sec:cohomology}

::: {#prop:cohomology .proposition}
Give every port the stalk $\mathbb{R}$ and every seam the identity restriction,
and let $\delta$ be the resulting coboundary. Then the sheaf has
$\dim H^{0} = {{SHEAF_H0}}$ up to a constant, $\dim H^{1} = {{SHEAF_H1}}$, and
the coboundary has rank {{SHEAF_COBOUNDARY_RANK}}, so the Euler characteristic of
the complex is {{SHEAF_EULER_CHARACTERISTIC}}.
:::

The probe reports the coboundary's shape and rank, the two cohomology dimensions,
the Euler characteristic, and the dimensions of the same quantities for a chain
sheaf used as a control.

The two numbers have concrete readings, which is the reason for reporting them
separately rather than as one identity. The global sections are the assignments
of equal values to adjacent ports, so $\dim H^{0} = {{SHEAF_H0}}$ says that the only way for
every seam to agree is for every port to carry the same value: the agreement is a
constant. The cohomology $\dim H^{1} = {{SHEAF_H1}}$ counts the seam assignments that
cannot be written as the coboundary of any port assignment, and its value is the
cycle rank of the seam graph, $\lvert E\rvert - \lvert V\rvert + 1 = {{CARRIER_SEAMS}} - {{CARRIER_PORTS}} + 1$.
So the carrier's frustration space is one-dimensional in the constants and
nineteen-dimensional in the disagreements, and both dimensions are forced by the
graph rather than chosen [@hansen2019sheaves; @ghrist2014elementary].

## The symmetry group closes the census {#sec:symmetry}

::: {#prop:symmetry .proposition}
The automorphism group of the carrier has order {{SYMMETRY_GROUP_ORDER}}, is
closed under composition, and acts transitively on the {{CARRIER_PORTS}} ports,
the {{CARRIER_SEAMS}} seams and the {{CARRIER_FACES}} faces. It splits into
{{SYMMETRY_ROTATIONS}} rotations and {{SYMMETRY_REFLECTIONS}} reflections, and
the Laplacian eigenspaces of dimensions {{SYMMETRY_EIGENSPACE_DIMENSIONS}} are irreducible under
it. Across all group elements and all {{SYMMETRY_CONFIGURATIONS}} total-two
configurations, the multiset of descent outcomes takes
{{SYMMETRY_OUTCOME_MULTISETS}} values, on {{SYMMETRY_ORBITS}} orbits of
configurations.
:::

The probe reports each part separately: the group order from an exhaustive
backtracking search, closure under composition, the orbit decomposition of ports,
seams and faces, the split into rotations and reflections, the orbit ranks of the
three nontrivial eigenspaces against their dimensions, and the equivariance of
the descent law over the full product of ${{SYMMETRY_GROUP_ORDER}}$ group
elements and ${{SYMMETRY_CONFIGURATIONS}}$ configurations.

Three readings follow, and they are the reason this correspondence is in the
registry rather than in an appendix. First, the symmetry is a symmetry of the
*object*, so any claim about the carrier that is not group-invariant is a claim
about a labelling. Second, the eigenspaces being irreducible means the
multiplicities {{SYMMETRY_EIGENSPACE_DIMENSIONS}} are forced: they are the dimensions of the
icosahedral group's representations, so the spectrum's shape cannot be changed by
perturbing the graph without breaking the symmetry. Third, and most useful for
this paper, the descent census is equivariant: the multiset of outcomes is
preserved by every one of the group elements, and the probe searches the whole
product for a witness that would break it and finds none. The census numbers in
[@sec:pr01] are therefore invariants, and the {{SYMMETRY_ORBITS}} orbits are the
right unit of analysis. The registry carries this section as three entries:
`icosahedral-symmetry`, `eigenspace-irreducibility` and `law-equivariance`.

The rotation and reflection counts require the orientation of the faces, and that
detail is a correction worth reporting. Testing orientation by the set of
directed edges of a face fails on a closed surface, because such a set already
contains every edge in both directions; the test used here compares the cyclic
order of each face, rotated so that its smallest port leads, and only then does it
separate {{SYMMETRY_ROTATIONS}} rotations from
{{SYMMETRY_REFLECTIONS}} reflections.

## Information cost is the synergetic metric over two {#sec:kl-metric}

::: {#prop:kl .proposition}
For two equal-covariance Gaussians centred at the origin and at a lattice site
$z$, the Kullback–Leibler divergence is half the squared norm of the separation,

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(0, I) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(z, I)\bigr)
\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z\rVert^{2} ,
$$ {#eq:kl}

and the IVM's first neighbour shell sits at squared norm
${{KL_STEP_SQUARED_DISTANCE}}$, so one step costs
${{KL_ONE_STEP}}$ nats and the sites at squared norm $8k^{2}$ cost $4k^{2}$.
:::

The probe reports the divergence for each of the twelve neighbour moves, the
squared distance of a move, and the sequence of costs for the first four shells.

This is the shortest of the identifications and the one with the widest reach,
because it is the only place in the registry where the FEP's own currency and
Synergetics' own metric are the same function. The lattice supplies the distances;
the divergence turns them into costs; and because the IVM's squared distances are
integers, the costs are half-integers, which is why a table of information costs of
lattice sites is an integer table scaled by two. It also fixes a convention that
the paper uses everywhere else: a "one-step cost" is a divergence, not an energy,
and the two differ by the factor of two that [@eq:kl] makes explicit.

## The carrier's subgraphs are the solid's own counts {#sec:census}

::: {#prop:subgraph-census .proposition}
Counting the seam graph's subgraphs by exhaustion returns
{{CARRIER_TRIANGLES}} triangles, {{CARRIER_PENTAGONS}} five-cycles,
{{CARRIER_PERFECT_MATCHINGS}} perfect matchings and
{{CARRIER_HAMILTONIAN_CYCLES}} labelled Hamiltonian cycles, and the first of
those is exactly the number of faces the port chart was derived from.
:::

The probe enumerates rather than quotes: every three-vertex subset for the
triangles, every five-vertex subset for the pentagons, and a memoized recursion
for the matchings and the Hamiltonian cycles, all from the adjacency matrix
alone. The triangle count is where two derivations meet. The carrier's faces were
*derived* from OPH's oriented base faces in [@sec:port-chart], and the census then
*rediscovers* {{CARRIER_TRIANGLES}} triangles in the graph that derivation
produced. A count that agreed by construction would prove nothing; this one is
computed from the adjacency and checked against the face list afterwards.

The five-cycles are the dual solid's vertices, which is the first place in the
paper where the dodecahedron appears without being named. The perfect matchings
and the Hamiltonian cycles have no counterpart in any of the three programmes,
and that is the reason to report them: they are properties of the object all
three are about, available to anyone who can read its adjacency.

## Curvature is exact in units of pi {#sec:defect}

::: {#prop:defect .proposition}
Five equilateral triangles meet at every port, so each port carries an angle
defect of {{CARRIER_DEFECT_PER_PORT}} pi and the twelve ports carry
{{CARRIER_TOTAL_DEFECT}} pi in total, which is $2\pi\chi$ for a triangulated
sphere: the combinatorial form of Gauss-Bonnet, read off the face list.
:::

The probe computes each defect as twice pi minus the incident faces times pi over
three and returns it as an exact rational multiple of pi rather than as a decimal:
{{CARRIER_DEFECT_PER_PORT}} at each port, {{CARRIER_TOTAL_DEFECT}} in total,
against the Euler characteristic that [@sec:census] has already reached from the
other direction. The identity is the reason twelve five-fold vertices close into
a sphere rather than a dish, and it is exact for the same reason the tree number
is: rational quantities over a finite complex. Registered as `angle-defect`.

## What the seventeen agreements do not say

None of the seventeen claims identifies OPH's *dynamics* with the FEP's
*functional*. Several of them are statements about counting, and most of the rest
are statements about the carrier, its spectrum, its repair rule as an integer map,
the Gaussian free energy on it, and the sheaf whose Laplacian it is. Every one of
those is a statement about a finite object that both programmes can be given.

The remaining identifications are structural rather than numerical, and the paper
reports them with the same care: a symmetry group of order
{{SYMMETRY_GROUP_ORDER}} is exact arithmetic, a spanning-tree number is an exact
integer, and a cohomology dimension is an exact rank. What none of them supplies
is a claim that either programme's *learning* dynamics is the other's.

The next section reports the fifteen places where the identification fails, and
most of them fail for one reason: the authors of each programme attach a
*dynamical* claim to that shared object, and the dynamical claims do not agree.
